Course overview

This course covers the foundations and modern frontiers of Natural Language Processing (NLP), with a heavy emphasis on Large Language Models (LLMs). You will learn the modern pipeline for building an LLM: tokenization, language modeling, the Transformer, pretraining, evaluation, post-training, retrieval, alignment, efficiency, and agents.

This edition's storyline: one denoiser, many corruptions

A language model is a learned reverse process. Pick a way to corrupt a token sequence (the forward process), train a network to undo one step of it (the denoiser), and run the reverse process from pure corruption back to text (the sampler). Every model in this course is that triple with a different corruption. The NLP content is the same as before; the story that connects it is new.

Three facts carry the argument, and each gets its own in-class demonstration: BERT's loss is one step of absorbing-state diffusion (Week 8); a left-to-right LM is masked diffusion with a fixed unmasking order (Week 8); and post-training is the same recipe for both families, with SFT as denoising on curated data and RLHF/GRPO/DPO as reward-tilted reverse processes (Week 10). You will build one small code base with three functions, corrupt(x, t), denoiser(x_t, t, cond), and sample(denoiser, cond). Week 5's Transformer is the denoiser. Every later week changes corrupt or sample, never the network.

Who this course is for

  • Prerequisites: no formal prerequisites. For Fudan students, it is safe to take this course in your second year. The core needs only expectation, KL divergence, Bayes' rule, Markov chains, and Jensen's inequality, all reviewed in Week 1.
  • Mixed audience: senior undergraduates and master's students share the room. Lectures use discrete time and discrete states in the core. Continuous time (SDEs, CTMCs, score entropy, flow matching) lives in the ★ layer and in Week 13.
★ Graduate layer. The last 25–30 minutes of each block go deeper while undergraduates start the exercise. ★ material is never on a quiz. Every assignment has a ★ part: required and worth 25% for master's students, a bonus worth up to 5% for undergraduates.

Project (40%)

Individual work inside three-person pods. Details below.

Course project

One project per student, reviewed inside a pod of three (one master's student and two undergraduates), with a short oral defence. What we assess is judgement: choosing the question, designing the evaluation, and deciding what to keep from what your coding assistant proposed.

Track A · Build

Build a small chat model under a fixed budget: tokenizer → pretrain → SFT → RL → evaluation, following nanochat's stages. Either an autoregressive model or a masked diffusion LM.

Track B · Reproduce and extend

Reproduce one main table from a 2025–26 paper and extend it with an ablation, a new dataset, or a new setting.

Every project must include something an assistant cannot supply

Choose at least one: a dataset you collected yourself; a Chinese-language angle; a reproduction of a table from a 2025–26 paper with a measured discrepancy; or a negative result, explained.

Deliverables

  1. Pods formed (Week 5).
  2. One-page proposal (Week 7): problem, dataset, baselines.
  3. Baseline reproduction (Week 9).
  4. Pod draft review (Week 10): each pod reviews its members' drafts and writes one shared comparison page relating its three projects.
  5. Poster session (Week 12): preliminary results, pods present side by side, structured peer review.
  6. Oral defence (Weeks 15–16): 8 minutes per student during exercise time, with the instructor and TAs.
  7. Final report (Week 17): at most 4 pages of main content in the ACL template, unlimited appendix, plus code and a one-page decision log: what the assistant proposed, what you rejected, and why. The log is graded.

What good looks like

  • Undergraduates: a careful reproduction, one extension, an honest evaluation, the decision log, and the defence.
  • Master's students: an original question with an ablation, one ★ component (a continuous-time formulation, a sampler analysis, or a diffusion-vs-AR comparison), and reviewer duty for the pod.

Each student receives a fixed GPU-hour budget on the university cluster. Small models are the point of this course, not a limitation.
