## What the evidence says

### Question 1: Does the tokenizer change model quality?

Yes. The effect is small on English tasks and large for other languages.

1. The best vocabulary grows with compute. At 3B parameters and equal FLOPs, going from 32K to 43K entries raised ARC-Challenge from 29.1 to 32.0 ([Scaling Laws with Vocabulary](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/93395), NeurIPS 2024).
2. It matters most outside English. Tokenizer choice barely moved English tasks but changed multilingual results consistently, and small models predicted the effect on large ones ([Beyond Text Compression](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1546/), ACL 2025). An English-centric tokenizer cost multilingual models up to 68% more training compute ([Tokenizer Choice For LLM Training](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-naacl.247/), Findings of NAACL 2024).
3. Fewer tokens is not the same as better. Across 64 models from 350M to 2.4B parameters, compression did not predict quality ([Tokenization Is More Than Compression](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.40/), EMNLP 2024). The pre-tokenizer had the biggest effect ([Tokenization is Sensitive to Language Variation](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.572/), Findings of ACL 2025).
4. Letting tokens cross spaces helps English: +4.0% averaged over 30 tasks and +8.2 on MMLU, at the same model and vocabulary size ([SuperBPE](https://openreview.net/forum?id=LwTWkSXIpt), COLM 2025). It may not carry over to Chinese: a later study found SuperBPE matched BPE on English but was 0.01–0.06 bits per byte worse on Mandarin ([Benchmarking BPE Tokenizers with Bits per Byte](https://aclanthology.org/2026.mellm-1.27/), MeLLM workshop at ACL 2026).
5. For numbers, one digit per token was more data-efficient than 2- or 3-digit tokens when training from scratch ([Scaling Behavior for LLMs regarding Numeral Systems](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.218/), Findings of EMNLP 2024).
6. The cleanest test so far trains 14 models that differ only in their tokenizer, with a Chinese robustness benchmark ([TokSuite](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/oral/71141), ICML 2026 oral).

### Question 2: What should the tokenizer be trained on?

1. The same cleaned text the model trains on. When tokenizer data and model data differ, the vocabulary keeps tokens the model never learns ([Fishing for Magikarp](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.649/), EMNLP 2024).
2. Filter Chinese web text first: more than 23% of long Chinese tokens in GPT vocabularies are porn or gambling terms ([Speculating LLMs' Chinese Training Data Pollution](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1327/), EMNLP 2025).
3. Normalize with care. Merging near-duplicate tokens such as "now" and "Now" hurt model quality, though the loss was easy to reduce ([On the Effect of (Near) Duplicate Subwords](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.571/), Findings of ACL 2024). Always applying Unicode normalization removed signal that language classification needed ([From Bytes to Subwords](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.530/), Findings of ACL 2026).
4. Enough, not more: gains flatten near 150 GB of English and 200 GB of Russian text ([How Much is Enough?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20273), arXiv 2025, not peer-reviewed).
5. Choose the language mix with cheap proxy tokenizers; this beat a LLaMA-3-style mix by up to 12% in compression ([TReX](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.298/), EACL 2026).
6. Decide before pretraining. Switching a trained model to a new tokenizer took more than 50B tokens of further training for code models ([Getting the most out of your tokenizer](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/dagan24a.html), ICML 2024). Embedding-transfer methods now close most of the gap far sooner ([Zero-Shot Tokenizer Transfer](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/532ce4fcf853023c4cf2ac38cbc5d002-Abstract-Conference.html), NeurIPS 2024).

## Read first

- [TReX: Tokenizer Regression for Optimal Data Mixture](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.298/), EACL 2026. A regression fit on small proxy tokenizers predicts the best language mixture, beating LLaMA3-based and uniform mixtures by up to 12% in compression.
- [TokSuite: Measuring the Impact of Tokenizer Choice on Language Model Behavior](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/oral/71141), ICML 2026 (oral). Fourteen models identical except for the tokenizer, plus a robustness benchmark in English, Chinese, Farsi, Italian and Turkish written by native speakers, isolate the tokenizer's effect.
- [Beyond Text Compression: Evaluating Tokenizers Across Scales](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1546/), ACL 2025. Small models predict tokenizer effects on large ones; tokenizer choice barely matters for English tasks but matters consistently in multilingual settings, where Zipf-based metrics predict better than compression.
- [Explaining and Mitigating Crosslingual Tokenizer Inequities](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/loc/san-diego/poster/120308), NeurIPS 2025. Across about 7,000 tokenizers for 97 languages, vocabulary size and pre-tokenization (not train/test data similarity) drive the extra token cost of a language; a best vocabulary size per language and superword tokenizers reduce it.
- [How Much is Enough? The Diminishing Returns of Tokenization Training Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20273), arXiv only (2025). BPE, UnigramLM and WordPiece tokenizers were trained on 1GB–900GB of English; gains level off after about 150GB (about 200GB for Russian), and the authors blame pre-tokenization limits.
- [Large Vocabulary Size Improves Large Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.57/), Findings of ACL 2025. Larger vocabularies gave better LLMs in English and Japanese, and switching to a new vocabulary for a new target language beat keeping the original one.
- [Over-Tokenized Transformer: Vocabulary is Generally Worth Scaling](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/huang25bb.html), ICML 2025. With separate input and output vocabularies, training loss falls log-linearly as the input (multi-gram) vocabulary grows; a large input vocabulary matches a baseline twice the size at no extra cost.
- [Speculating LLMs' Chinese Training Data Pollution from Their Tokens](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1327/), EMNLP 2025. In GPT vocabularies, more than 23% of long Chinese tokens (3+ characters) are porn or gambling terms; the related pages are estimated at about 0.5% of GPT-4o's training data.
- [SuperBPE: Space Travel for Language Models](https://openreview.net/forum?id=LwTWkSXIpt), COLM 2025. Letting BPE merge across spaces uses up to 33% fewer tokens (200k vocabulary); an 8B model gained +4.0% on average over 30 tasks (+8.2% on MMLU) with 27% less inference compute.
- [Fishing for Magikarp: Automatically Detecting Under-trained Tokens in Large Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.649/), EMNLP 2024. Automatic detection shows that under-trained ("glitch") vocabulary tokens are common across many LLMs.
- [Scaling Laws with Vocabulary: Larger Models Deserve Larger Vocabularies](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/93395), NeurIPS 2024. The best vocabulary size grows with compute: Llama2-70B should have used at least 216K tokens (7× its 32K), and moving from 32K to 43K raised ARC-Challenge from 29.1 to 32.0 at the same 2.3e21 FLOPs.
- [Tokenization Is More Than Compression](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.40/), EMNLP 2024. Across 64 LMs (350M–2.4B), a tokenizer built to use the fewest tokens (PathPiece) did not score better downstream; pre-tokenization and a BPE-initialized vocabulary mattered more.
- [Tokenizer Choice For LLM Training: Negligible or Crucial?](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-naacl.247/), Findings of NAACL 2024. Across 24 LLMs at 2.6B, tokenizer choice significantly changed quality and cost; English-centric tokenizers in multilingual models hurt performance and added up to 68% training cost; fertility and parity did not reliably predict results.

## How this list was made

Six search passes ran on 2026-09-12. Three broad passes covered NLP venues (ACL, EMNLP, NAACL, EACL, COLING, TACL, COLM), ML venues (NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR), and work on tokenizer training data at any venue. A 2026 pass then scanned every title on the ACL 2026, EACL 2026 and LREC 2026 Anthology event pages (workshops included), the TACL and Computational Linguistics 2026 volumes, the ICML 2026 and ICLR 2026 main tracks, and TMLR 2026. A last pass targeted Chinese tokenization, digits, and cleaning of tokenizer data. Each venue was confirmed on an official page (ACL Anthology, conference proceedings, PMLR, or accepted-paper lists). 21 papers had no official page and are marked "arXiv only"; workshop papers say so. Findings paraphrase each paper's abstract; every claim in "What the evidence says" was re-read in the paper's abstract.

### Known gaps

1. COLM 2026 is incomplete: only 200 of its 856 papers were public without a login.
2. The ACL Anthology had no NAACL 2026 or EMNLP 2026 volume yet, NeurIPS 2026 decisions were not public, and AAAI 2026 could not be reached.
3. Evidence is thin on three questions that matter for our model:
   - cleaning or normalizing the tokenizer's training text (no peer-reviewed paper isolates deduplication before tokenizer training);
   - word- versus character-level Chinese vocabularies in decoder models;
   - pre-tokenizing LaTeX and math.
4. Every search pass hit its search limit, so the list is not complete.
