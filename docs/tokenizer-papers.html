What should our tokenizer be?

Research from 2022 to September 2026 on two questions for our 1.1B English–Chinese model: does the tokenizer change model quality, and what text should the tokenizer be trained on. Every venue below was checked on its official page; papers found only on arXiv are marked.

We choose the tokenizer once, before 50B tokens of 训练。

Question one

Does the tokenizer change model quality?

  • Yes, and the best vocabulary grows with compute. At 3B parameters and equal FLOPs, going from 32K to 43K entries raised ARC-Challenge from 29.1 to 32.0. NeurIPS 2024
  • It matters most outside English. Tokenizer choice barely moved English tasks but changed multilingual results consistently, and small models predicted the effect on large ones. An English-centric tokenizer cost multilingual models up to 68% more training compute. ACL 2025 Findings NAACL 2024
  • Fewer tokens is not the same as better. Across 64 models from 350M to 2.4B parameters, compression did not predict quality; the pre-tokenizer had the biggest effect. EMNLP 2024 Findings ACL 2025
  • Letting tokens cross spaces helps English: +4.0% averaged over 30 tasks and +8.2 on MMLU, at the same model and vocabulary size. COLM 2025 It may not carry over to Chinese: a later study found SuperBPE matched BPE on English but was 0.01–0.06 bits per byte worse on Mandarin. ACL 2026 workshop
  • For numbers, one digit per token was more data-efficient than 2- or 3-digit tokens when training from scratch. Findings EMNLP 2024
  • The cleanest test so far trains 14 models that differ only in their tokenizer, with a Chinese robustness benchmark. ICML 2026
Question two

What should the tokenizer be trained on?

  • The same cleaned text the model trains on. When tokenizer data and model data differ, the vocabulary keeps tokens the model never learns. EMNLP 2024
  • Filter Chinese web text first: more than 23% of long Chinese tokens in GPT vocabularies are porn or gambling terms. EMNLP 2025
  • Normalize with care. Merging near-duplicate tokens hurt model quality, though the loss was easy to reduce, and always applying Unicode normalization removed signal that language classification needed. Findings ACL 2024 Findings ACL 2026
  • Enough, not more: gains flatten near 150 GB of English and 200 GB of Russian text. arXiv 2025
  • Choose the language mix with cheap proxy tokenizers; this beat a LLaMA-3-style mix by up to 12% in compression. EACL 2026
  • Decide before pretraining. Switching a trained model to a new tokenizer took more than 50B tokens of further training for code models, though embedding-transfer methods now close most of the gap far sooner. ICML 2024 NeurIPS 2024

The papers

How this list was made. Six search passes ran on September 12, 2026. Three broad passes covered NLP venues (ACL, EMNLP, NAACL, EACL, COLING, TACL, COLM), ML venues (NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR), and work on tokenizer training data at any venue. A 2026 pass then scanned every title on the ACL 2026, EACL 2026 and LREC 2026 Anthology event pages (workshops included), the TACL and Computational Linguistics 2026 volumes, the ICML 2026 and ICLR 2026 main tracks, and TMLR 2026. A last pass targeted Chinese tokenization, digits, and cleaning of tokenizer data. Each venue was confirmed on an official page: the ACL Anthology, NeurIPS or ICLR proceedings, PMLR or icml.cc, or the COLM accepted-paper lists. Papers with no official page are marked "arXiv only". Workshop papers say so, and some COLM links point to arXiv because OpenReview blocked the automated check. Each finding paraphrases the paper's abstract. Every claim in the two summary panels was re-read in the paper's own abstract, on the anthology, proceedings, or arXiv page.

Known gaps. COLM 2026 is incomplete: only 200 of its 856 papers were public without a login. The ACL Anthology had no NAACL 2026 or EMNLP 2026 volume yet, NeurIPS 2026 decisions were not public, and AAAI 2026 could not be reached. Evidence is thin on three questions: cleaning or normalizing the tokenizer's training text (no peer-reviewed paper isolates deduplication before tokenizer training), word- versus character-level Chinese vocabularies in decoder models, and pre-tokenizing LaTeX. Every pass hit its search limit, so the list is not complete. "Read first" marks the papers the search passes judged most useful for choosing the tokenizer of a 1B English–Chinese model.
