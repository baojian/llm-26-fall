Choose a question you can test

Each card is a proposed investigation, not a promise that a paper's results will reproduce at this scale. The minimum outcome is designed for one student over the remaining semester, with roughly 4-6 hours of work per week. A well-supported negative result is a complete outcome. All students have the same requirements and maximum score under the published course policy.

Start small: one main dataset or generator, one model family, one baseline, and one controlled change. Freeze a development/evaluation split and record model revisions, prompts, seeds, budgets, and unsuccessful runs. Inspect at least 20 examples when the dataset permits. Use repeated runs or uncertainty estimates appropriate to the experiment. Reusing assignment code is welcome; identify the project's additional question and evidence.

Preparation: the first four lectures provide text processing, n-grams, evaluation, embeddings, PyTorch, and neural/recurrent models. Each card names what remains to learn. Attention, Transformers, tuning, retrieval, diffusion, and agents can be learned from the linked starter resources before their later lectures.

Compute estimates are planning targets, not allocations or measured runtimes. CPU, inference, training, and external-review labels describe resources, not grading tracks. Pilot 10 examples or 100 training steps, then reduce scope if needed. Paid APIs are optional; propose a spending cap before using them. Small-model projects should usually begin at 0.6B-2B for inference, 0.6B for adapter tuning, or 5M-30M when training from scratch.

How this updates the earlier project list

The Spring 2026 list combined older challenge tasks with recent papers. Standalone reproductions of SemEval-2014 sentiment classification, the Fake News Challenge, 2019/2020 commonsense tasks, and generic Kaggle classification are retired from this shortlist. Their datasets and simple methods can still serve as useful baselines.

BLT, sparse attention, difference-aware fairness, cross-lingual intervention, formal-language pretraining, and Infini-gram mini remain useful after reducing their scope. The reasoning-faithfulness project adds a 2026 control study. New directions include editable context, agent memory and security, Qwen-Scope, small diffusion checkpoints, current multimodal models, and upstream benchmark contributions. Foundational methods may be older; every project asks a current, bounded question.

Contribute something other people can use

Instructor direction, October 9: substantive individual work accepted and merged into established, actively used open-source repositories will be considered for additional course-project credit. The exact points, cap, and treatment within or beyond the existing project maximum have not yet been specified. No automatic bonus is assigned by this catalog; the published rubric remains the basis for grading until a common policy is announced.

A new benchmark task, a verified evaluator repair, a useful model adapter, or a reusable dataset/tool can be a strong project. Agree on a useful contribution with upstream maintainers early. Keep a local experiment and report that answer the project question even if review takes longer than the semester.

For credit consideration, include the upstream PR and merge-commit links, maintainer acceptance, your individual contribution, tests and before/after evidence, and how review changed the work. A self-merged personal fork, raw PR count, or repository star count is not evidence of substantive external acceptance. This guidance does not promise a maintainer decision or paper authorship.
