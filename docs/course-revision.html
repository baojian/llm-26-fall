Course progress and the remaining six lectures

Current progress: November 10, 2026

Weeks 8 and 9 reflect the actual teaching: InstructGPT/SFT/reward models, then REINFORCE/PPO. GRPO and DPO remain prepared supplementary material, not topics already taught. Week 10 combines data preparation and quality with evaluation and experimental design. The current schedule and notes index are the active references. The next six meetings are Lectures 11–16. They advance from the taught post-training foundations to preference optimization, retrieval, inference, diffusion, agents and research synthesis. Existing assessment dates and weights remain unchanged.

Lectures 01–10 have slide and notebook packages. The sections below are preparation plans for Lectures 11–16, not a claim that six new decks or demos are ready. Lecture 10 has 45 core slides, five offline exercises and an optional appendix; its teaching plan reserves 15 minutes for Quiz 4 after 120 minutes of instruction.

The schedule adjustment proposal records the approved assessment dates and the October 9 proposal-deadline extension. The course page and dated schedule carry the matching sequence. All planning dates and class times use Asia/Shanghai (UTC+08:00).

Design decisions

  • Assume Python and an introductory machine-learning course. Teach the PyTorch details needed for the common exercises, and provide a preparation notebook.
  • Plan 16 teaching meetings. The Week 5 meeting moves from October 7 to Saturday, October 10. Teach Transformers there and advance later topics by one meeting. Use Week 16 for research synthesis and project revision.
  • Retain NLP foundations and the useful spring notebooks. Add explicit data, training, resource-accounting, and evaluation work from CS336.
  • Use one individual project per student. No presentations, posters, oral defenses, assigned project pods, or shared group deliverables are required.
  • Allow substantial AI assistance. Grade the question, design, evidence, interpretation, and reproducibility. More experimental iteration is encouraged; code volume and application feature count are not learning objectives.
  • Keep diffusion as one late course session and an advanced project option. Remove the requirement to interpret every early topic as a denoising process.
  • Write teaching materials in English, following the repository guidelines.

Teaching sequence

Each meeting has 135 minutes of instruction, excluding breaks. The default is three 45-minute periods: concepts/worked example, experiment/interpretation, and guided practice. Quizzes take 15 minutes from practice and cover material already taught. Advanced work is an extension choice rather than a second mandatory curriculum delivered during every meeting.

Lecture 11: DPO, GRPO and verifiable rewards

November 18 · 135 minutes. When can a preference or reward improve the behavior we actually care about? Start from the taught SFT/RM/PPO objectives and Lecture 10's independent evaluation boundary.

Period Teaching route Student evidence
1, 45 min From a reference-relative preference margin to the DPO loss; distinguish offline pairs from on-policy samples Compute one loss and explain which response is pushed up relative to the reference
2, 45 min GRPO: sample a response group, compare rewards, normalize advantages, retain the PPO ratio and clipping distinction Calculate group advantages; diagnose an all-equal-reward group and its zero learning signal
3, 45 min Verifiable rewards, incomplete tests and reward exploitation; compare DPO/PPO/GRPO data requirements Audit a toy verifier and specify independent evaluation, token budget and failure slices

Read DPO, Sections 3–4 and DeepSeekMath, Section 4. Reward model overoptimization is an optional empirical case. Reuse Lecture 09's DPO/GRPO supplements as source material; they have not been taught. Prepare a CPU arithmetic notebook and a verifier counterexample before adding optional live sampling. Full RL training is not needed for these learning objectives.

Lecture 12: Retrieval and RAG

Period Teaching route Student evidence

2, 45 min Chunk boundaries, reranking and context assembly under a fixed budget Compare two chunking or retrieval policies on the same questions

Lecture 13: Inference and computation at test time

December 2 · 135 minutes. How should a fixed inference budget be spent? Connect Lecture 07's compute allocation to Lecture 10's protocol and Lecture 11's verifier. Separate systems efficiency from spending more attempts.

Period Teaching route Student evidence
1, 45 min Prefill, decoding and KV-cache size; latency versus throughput; attention I/O A dimension-checked cache-memory estimate and a workload definition
2, 45 min Batching, cache reuse and quantization; warm-up and repeated measurements A correctness-checked timing/memory comparison with model, hardware and token counts
3, 45 min Best-of-N, majority vote and verification; actual selection versus oracle pass@k Plot success against attempts, total tokens and wall time; state selection cost and limits

Read FlashAttention, Sections 2–3, PagedAttention/vLLM, Sections 3–4, and Snell et al., test-time compute. Prepare a small CPU cache example and auditable candidate traces. An optional GPU profile must report its own hardware; it cannot turn a CPU trace into a measured GPU speedup. A3 keeps its published deadline that evening.

Lecture 14: Masked diffusion language models

December 9 · 120 teaching minutes + 15 minutes for Quiz 5. What changes when a generator can update multiple positions? Retrieve BERT's masked prediction objective and the causal decoder as distinct starting points.

Period Teaching route Student evidence
1, 45 min A discrete masking process and time-dependent denoising loss Trace one corruption/target pair; explain why BERT alone is not a complete diffusion generator
2, 45 min Iterative unmasking, conditioning and sampling schedules Trace token states through a small sampler and identify its stopping rule
3, 30 min + quiz Compare quality and cost against autoregression; then Quiz 5 State step count, token budget, model differences and measured timing limits

Read MDLM, Sections 3–4 and LLaDA, Sections 2–3. Prepare a CPU toy sampler with explicit assumptions. Updating positions in parallel does not establish an end-to-end speedup; report repeated passes and actual runtime. Quiz 5 covers previously taught core material, not new diffusion content or optional extensions; its questions remain in the instructor repository.

Lecture 15: Agents and tool use

December 16 · 135 minutes. When is a tool-use loop worth its additional cost? Extend a direct-answer baseline into a bounded observe–act–tool loop.

Period Teaching route Student evidence
1, 45 min State, tool schema, observations and stopping conditions Trace one successful episode and one failed tool call
2, 45 min Tool permissions, untrusted tool text, recovery and action limits Inspect a prompt-injection fixture and distinguish an observation from an authorized instruction
3, 45 min Direct generation versus a bounded agent on the same tasks Report success, failures, tool calls, tokens, latency and stopping behavior

Read ReAct, Sections 2–3. SWE-bench is an optional evaluation-design case, not a required full benchmark run. Prepare an offline tool environment and replayable traces; network access and paid APIs are unnecessary for the core lesson. Training large agents or coordinating many agents is optional reading.

Individual projects and AI assistance

Students choose a meaningful uncertainty rather than merely a list of features to implement. Building a system is welcome when it enables the investigation. The proposal establishes importance, prior work, plausible alternatives, evaluation, and feasibility. Careful replication, useful failure analysis, or a sound negative result can earn full credit.

Written milestones are Week 7 proposal, Week 12 progress update (including a baseline and initial results), and Week 17 final report. These replace the former posters, oral defenses, pod comparison pages, and mandatory reviewer roles. Students can reuse and revise checkpoint text in the final report. Peer discussion and individual office-hour consultations remain available.

AI assistance is allowed for implementation, engineering, research exploration, and iteration. Students verify sources, check computations, inspect examples, and own their conclusions. Keep a concise record of important decisions and traceable experiments; do not require a full chat transcript or an artificial list of rejected AI suggestions. Quizzes and designated concept checks assess individual understanding without AI.

The tiny model trained in A2 supports learning about pretraining. A supplied pretrained checkpoint supports SFT and application experiments. Projects on later topics can start from a supplied baseline; students need not wait for the relevant lecture or depend on their own early training run succeeding.

Assessment context and workload

They establish common skills and can provide infrastructure for the project; the final project identifies its additional question and experiments rather than receiving duplicate credit for the same submission.
